# Design

## Context

The desktop's `legview` places every leg in the body frame from the pose frame's commanded angles (forward kinematics, `Mounts` with schematic coxa pivots), and the four-view window draws the result. The host tests of the gaits and gestures check that the body's centre (the body frame's origin) stays inside the polygon of the feet on the ground, with their own copies of the schematic pivots. Nothing models mass.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- An estimate of the centre of gravity for any reported pose, shown where the operator already looks at the whole robot.
- One model, in `core/`, that host tests of gestures can use, with the desktop's copy pinned to it.

**Non-Goals:**
- Dynamics: accelerations and foot forces are ignored. The estimate is static; it fits slow gaits and gestures and is optimistic for Hop and Bi.
- Sending the estimate from the robot. The desktop computes it from the pose frame; the protocol is unchanged.
- Real body geometry (`add-gestures` task 2.1) and measured masses. Both replace values in place.

## Decisions

- **Point masses** (grams; the servo and the battery weighed, the rest estimates):
  - body without battery: 420 (printed frame, Servo 2040, BEC, wiring), at the body frame's origin;
  - battery, 2S 5000 mAh: 284 (weighed), at the origin;
  - per leg: three MG996R servos at 55 each; the coxa servo at the coxa pivot, the femur servo at the femur joint (the coxa's end), the tibia servo at the knee; the coxa link (30) halfway along the coxa, the femur link (45) halfway along the femur, the tibia link (50) 40% of the way from the knee to the foot.
  
  The servo placement follows the upstream design's joints; the link weights are guesses for PLA parts. Without the battery the robot is estimated at 2.16 kg, 1.74 kg of it in the legs.
- **The model lives in `core/movement/centre_of_gravity`**, with its own forward kinematics (the inverse of `solveLeg`, as `legview` has it) and the schematic pivots, which `add-gestures` 2.1 later replaces with the real mounts. The firmware does not compile it.
- **The support polygon is the convex hull** of the feet within 10 mm of the lowest foot (the gesture test's contact rule). With fewer than three such feet there is no polygon and no margin. The margin is the signed distance from the centre of gravity's ground projection to the nearest hull edge, negative outside.
- **Margin colours in the window:** at least 20 mm (the host tests' margin) is shown as fine, 0–20 mm as a warning, outside as a danger.
- **The battery choice belongs to the window**, not the session: it changes only what is drawn, starts without the battery each time the window opens, and is not exported.
- **Golden file:** named poses (stands at three heights, the calibration pose, a lifted front leg, a walking snapshot), each with and without the battery: the 18 commanded angles, then the centre (x, y, z), the mass and the margin (or `none`). Generated by `test/test_centre_of_gravity` with `SPOODER_WRITE_GOLDEN=1`.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The estimated masses may be well off] → One table, named constants; the margin colours carry a 20 mm allowance. Weigh the parts when the robot is rebuilt.
- [Schematic pivots] → Same pivots as everything else that draws or tests the body; `add-gestures` 2.1 replaces them in one place for C++ and one for Go, with the golden file regenerated.
- [The battery's position in the body is not decided] → It sits at the origin; a constant moves it.
