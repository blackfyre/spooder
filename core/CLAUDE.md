# core/

Hardware-free C++ shared by both firmwares, the virtual robot and the host tests (`mise run test`). It includes only `<math.h>`/`<stdint.h>` (plus `<stdio.h>` for `snprintf` in `status/status_screen` and `robot/robot_tick`). The bridge compiles it under ESP-IDF, where POSIX `link()` and C++20 `std::lerp` are visible: hence `interpolate` in `movement/vectors.h`.

A change here reaches the robot, the bridge and the virtual robot: run `mise run build` (with and without `--serial`), `mise run build-bridge` and `mise run e2e` besides `mise run test` and `mise run lint` (formatting, and clang-tidy with `.clang-tidy`: fix a finding, or suppress it with `NOLINTNEXTLINE(check)` and a reason; never cast a narrowing away without the value's bound beside it).

- `movement/` (reference: the `spooder-leg-kinematics` skill):
  - `body`: the non-blocking state machine (Initialise, Stand, Walk, Sleep, Calibrate, Perform) on a fixed 5 ms internal grid.
  - `gesture`: the gestures Perform plays (say hi and wiggle so far): keyframe scripts of body shifts and lifted feet, and the player (eased, rate-limited segments; a stopped gesture finishes its keyframe, puts its feet down, then straightens). The scripts' joint angles are pinned by `test/golden/gestures.txt`, which the handheld's gesture table is generated from (`test/CLAUDE.md`).
  - `gait`, `kinematics` (IK, target validation, `servoOutputIndex`) and `vectors`.
  - `joint_calibration`: the per-joint calibration table (offset, 0° and 180° pulses, direction), its clamps, the 60°/s rate-limited `step` and `toPulse`.
  - `calibration_defaults.cpp`: the compiled defaults, in a fixed format that a front-end's export replaces whole. Its values are free to change; host tests check that it parses, stays within the guards and round-trips through the exporters.
  - `movement_command.h`: the request a controller hands to movement.
  - `body_geometry.h`: each leg's coxa pivot in the body frame and the body's underside (`UNDERSIDE_Z`, its lowest point below the pivots), measured from upstream's STLs; the mount angles are gait's. The desktop's `legview` copy is pinned by `test/golden/body_geometry.txt`.
  - `centre_of_gravity`: the estimated centre of gravity (point masses for the body, the battery and the legs' servos and links; weighed, the links' split of a leg an estimate, in one table), the support margin of the feet on the ground and `undersideGrounded` (the body rests on its frame, not its feet). Host tests only (the gesture test checks it); the desktop's `legview` has a copy.
  - `servo_profile.h`: the servo models (rated speed, share usable under load) and the build's choice, `SPOODER_SERVO_PROFILE` (default `MG996R`), which sets the joint speed budget. `body` slows its motion clock, in 20 ms pieces, to keep every joint within the budget; `robot_tick`'s output rate backstop never exceeds the rated speed.
- `protection/`: power protection and the emergency stop (and `SIMULATION_SUPPLY`, the simulation supply fault, latched from `protection::step`'s `simulationSupply` and never cleared while bench simulation holds it). It turns supply readings into "output allowed" and "force Sleep", with latched faults; `supervisor` decides when the body runs, stops or restarts.
- `protocol/`: the link's frames (version 3: command with the gesture request, telemetry with the gesture playing, calibration set and report, and the pose and diagnostics, sent on every link: the bridge relays them in WiFi drive, and keeps the diagnostics' protection limits for its supply bands), CRC and incremental decoder (both firmwares; `Decoder::frameBytes()` hands out a copy of the raw bytes of the frame `next()` last returned, which the bridge relays in WiFi drive without re-encoding), pinned by `test/golden/protocol_v3.txt`. `link_receiver` is the robot's handling of decoded frames: arrival stamping, clear, mode-request and gesture-request counters, baselines after a link gap, the calibration guards and reports, and the arbitration between the bridge and USB links. A protocol change bumps `protocol::VERSION`, the Go copy in `desktop/internal/protocol` and the golden file, and both boards are flashed together.
- `robot/robot_tick`: the robot's 20 ms control tick, shared by the firmware and the virtual robot: link decoding and arbitration (or the serial keys) → protection → supervisor → calibration sets → calibration step → `body::step` → servo output → LEDs → telemetry every fifth tick, the pose frame two ticks later and the diagnostics frame (motion share, backstop, tick time, limits) four ticks later, and calibration reports, all on both links → the status line. It logs the build banner on its first tick and whenever `Inputs::usbConnected` turns true. It drives the hardware through its `Hardware` interface. `Inputs::bench` (zero by default, as in the virtual robot) carries bench simulation into the tick: `TELEMETRY_SIMULATED` in every telemetry frame, the simulation supply fault to protection, the indication on `led_pattern::INPUT_LED` (LED 5 counted from 0, the command-source LED), `; SIMULATION` on the status line and the entered and refused log lines.
- `robot/bench_sim` (spec `bench-simulation`): bench simulation on the robot. `step()` is the entry decision, once per tick before `robot_tick::tick`: the 3 s window from the first tick, a press edge of the USER button, the supply gate (below 1.0 V on every tick since boot), the output gate (real output on closes the window), the USB gate at the press, one decision per boot, the refusal's reason (supply before USB) shown for 3 s, Simulating latched until restart, and in Simulating the supply fault once the measured supply exceeds the brown-out voltage. `BenchBoard` is the board boundary: a `robot_tick::Hardware` whose servo methods are `final` and reach the real servo port only through protected hooks; `enterSimulation()` sets a flag that is never cleared, calls `realForceOff` once, and routes every servo call to a `bench_model::Servos` from then on, with no path to `realApply`. Tested in `test_bench_sim`, with a fake board that fails on any real apply or un-inhibit after entry.
- `robot/bench_model`: the servo and supply model (`Servos`: `servo_output`'s rules for on, inhibit, stop and legs switching on in groups of two; `Supply`: a steady voltage and a current from the powered servos, the pulse change per tick and a load factor), shared by bench simulation (6.0 V, load 1) and the virtual robot, so the two cannot drift.
- `identity/build_identity`: the build identity's text, always printable ASCII: the console banner (`spooder <component>: build <id>`, `unknown` for an empty id, non-printable bytes as `?`, cut at 40 characters) and `fit` for the bridge screen (`..` and the id's end). `build_identity::ID` is declared here but defined only by the source `cmake/build_identity.cmake` generates in each firmware build; the host tests do not link it.
- `gamepad_mapping/`: gamepad input to the desired command state (bridge); `rumble`, the bridge's rumble feedback (fault, e-stop, link, refused gesture).
- `status/led_pattern`: what the robot's six status LEDs show; `status/status_screen`: the bridge screen's content (the drive, power and link pages with the status strip, in Bluetooth drive and WiFi drive, and the setup-mode text), with `status/power_history` (60 s of voltage and current), `status/page_button` (the left button as the page switch), `status/peak_hold` and `status/supply_band` (a voltage's or current's band, ok, warning or fault, against the limits the robot reports in its diagnostics frames, which the bridge keeps; the handheld's Go copy is pinned by `test/golden/supply_bands.txt`).
- `setup/`: the bridge's setup mode. `portal_controller` (portal polls and buttons as the controller), `calibration_sync` (pending edits, one set in flight, confirmation by report), `calibration_export` (a reported table as `calibration_defaults.cpp`), `setup_entry` (the button hold and the boot decision) and `access_point` (the setup and WiFi drive network names from one MAC, password, QR text).
- `wifi_drive/`: the bridge's WiFi drive. `drive_mode` (the boot decision from the setup request and the GPIO32 switch, setup winning; the switch notice, debounced 100 ms against the running mode), `handheld_controller` (the handheld's UDP datagrams as the controller: the 8-per-20 ms window budget, validation of exactly one version 3 command frame, the sender lock, the sequence rule with jumps ending the session, the 150 ms liveness, counter translation with Sleep until a session's first mode request, the e-stop flag kept through a lapse, the relay target and the outcome counts) and `relay_queue` (a ring of 8 raw robot frames that never waits, dropping and counting when full, cleared when the session changes, and, after a session change, dropping frames until a receive poll ends with the decoder empty).

A new `.cpp` here is listed by hand wherever it is built: the root `platformio.ini`'s `build_src_filter` (host tests, and the sources clang-tidy analyses; one `+<file>` entry per source, no globs: `mise run test` fails naming a file missing there, and `mise run lint` fails too), and as needed `firmware/servo2040/CMakeLists.txt`, `tools/virtual-robot/CMakeLists.txt` and `firmware/bridge/main/CMakeLists.txt`.
